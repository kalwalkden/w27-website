Why This Matters Now

Building is getting cheaper. Judgment isn’t.

AI can make a capable person or team dramatically faster. It can generate code, explore options, compress research, and reduce the cost of getting from an idea to an implementation.

That shifts the bottleneck. The harder questions are increasingly: What should we build? What should we leave alone? How should it fit the business? Which constraint actually matters? And how will we know whether the change worked?

I’ve spent my career working at that boundary between decision and execution. I use AI as leverage, but the value is still in choosing well, carrying the work through, and learning from what happens next.

How I Work

Decision. Execution. Evidence. Repeat.

Good judgment is not a one-time insight. Each decision creates new evidence, and that evidence should improve the next decision. The goal is a tight loop between understanding the problem and changing the result.

  1. Understand

    Start with the business or customer outcome that needs to change. Separate the symptoms from the constraint actually limiting it.

  2. Decide

    Make the tradeoffs explicit. Choose the smallest credible intervention that can change the outcome, and be clear about what will not be done.

  3. Execute

    Carry the decision into the product, architecture, team, process, or code. Keep intent and implementation connected as the work becomes real.

  4. Measure

    Use business, customer, reliability, flow, and capacity evidence to determine whether the intervention actually worked.

  5. Repeat

    Keep what worked, revise what did not, and feed the evidence into the next decision.
